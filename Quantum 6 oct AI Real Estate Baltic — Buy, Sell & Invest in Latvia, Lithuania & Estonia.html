Why Invest in the Baltics?

The Baltic states have consistently outperformed Western European real estate markets over the past decade. With EU membership, NATO security, the Euro currency, and a combined population of 6 million, Latvia, Lithuania and Estonia offer a rare combination of stability and growth.

Average annual price appreciation of 15% in Baltic capitals (2023–2025), combined with gross rental yields of 5–8%, makes this one of Europe's most compelling investment propositions.

EU citizens may purchase property in all three Baltic states with no restrictions. Non-EU citizens may purchase apartments and commercial properties freely; agricultural land and forests have additional requirements which our legal team handles fully.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can Baltic citizens buy property in France?

Yes — as EU citizens, Latvian, Lithuanian and Estonian nationals have the same rights as French citizens when purchasing property in France. There are no restrictions on the type of property you can buy. The process is fully open to Baltic nationals and we handle all the administrative requirements.

Do I need to travel to France to complete the purchase?

No — with a properly structured Power of Attorney, you can complete a French property purchase entirely remotely. We arrange virtual viewings, video calls with the notary, and handle all in-person requirements on your behalf. Many of our Baltic clients have purchased French properties without visiting France at all.

Can I get a French mortgage as a Baltic resident?

Yes — EU citizens are eligible for French mortgages. Baltic residents typically qualify for 70–80% loan-to-value. Current rates (2025) range from 3.5% to 4.2% depending on the lender and your profile. We work with specialist mortgage brokers who have experience with non-resident EU buyers and will negotiate the best terms for you.

Will I pay tax in both France and my Baltic country?

France has double taxation treaties with Latvia, Lithuania and Estonia — meaning you will not be taxed twice on the same income. Rental income from French property is taxed in France first. We advise on the most efficient tax structure for your specific situation, including whether an SCI company structure is beneficial.

How long does the French buying process take?

Typically 10–14 weeks from offer acceptance to completion. The main steps are: offer accepted (day 1), compromis signed (week 1–2), 10-day cooling-off period, mortgage arranged (weeks 3–8), final notarial deed (weeks 10–14). We manage this entire timeline and keep you informed at every stage in your language.

What is your fee for the legal & financial service?

The initial consultation is completely free. Our full legal and financial support package is charged as a percentage of the property purchase price (typically 1.5–2%), which covers all document preparation, translations, notary coordination, mortgage assistance and transaction management. This is discussed transparently at the consultation stage — no hidden fees ever.
